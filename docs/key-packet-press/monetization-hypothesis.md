# Monetization hypothesis

This document is a test plan, not a forecast, price commitment, or statement of current demand.
Key Packet Press has no buyer, payment, revenue, retention, or production-usage proof.

## Buyer hypothesis

The likely economic buyer is an independent hotel, small hotel group, or event-oriented property
whose front-office team repeatedly prepares packets for tours, weddings, teams, or conferences.
The daily operator may be a group-arrival coordinator, front-office manager, or group-sales
handoff owner.

The narrow value hypothesis is that a reliable local printable workflow can reduce manual sorting,
rework, and unnecessary open room-label exposure without requiring a PMS integration.

## Why payment might be plausible

Public qualification research shows recurring packet preparation, improvised physical
organization, compressed group-arrival throughput, and commercial hotel systems with adjacent
group-arrival features. That supports problem plausibility and commercial adjacency only. It does
not establish that a buyer wants this standalone product or will pay for it.

Potential paid value would need to come from repeated operational usefulness, support, controlled
branding and templates, documented offline deployment, and a broader set of privacy-aware
group-arrival print tools—not from selling or uploading guest data.

## Offers to test

Test one offer at a time with explicit privacy boundaries:

| Hypothesis | Illustrative price test | What must be learned |
|---|---:|---|
| Single-event utility | $9–$19 per event | Whether occasional coordinators value the artifact enough to pay once |
| Property license | $29–$49 per property per month | Whether repeated group arrivals create recurring use |
| Annual offline operations pack | $249–$399 per property per year | Whether local deployment, updates, templates, and support justify an annual purchase |

These ranges are research probes, not recommended or validated prices. Do not charge or describe
them as established until the buyer, purchasing authority, expected use frequency, and support
burden are known.

## Validation sequence

1. Interview at least ten reachable front-office or group-arrival operators about their last
   completed packet run, current workaround, error modes, frequency, and authority to buy.
2. Demonstrate only with synthetic data and ask the operator to narrate where the output fits or
   fails in the real workflow.
3. Run a permissioned concierge pilot without retaining real guest-room data.
4. Measure time to a ready print, corrections required, repeat use across separate arrivals, and
   whether the operator follows the index-handling workflow.
5. Present one explicit offer and record accept, decline, procurement blocker, and requested
   alternative without counting compliments as demand.
6. Add payment only after privacy, support, and release-integrity requirements are defined.

## Decision signals

Evidence supporting continued investment would include:

- repeated use for separate group arrivals;
- a named budget owner;
- a paid pilot or signed purchase intent at a tested price;
- measured reduction in assembly time or rework;
- willingness to adopt the fold-and-index handling procedure; and
- demand for adjacent local-first print workflows from the same buyer.

Pause or narrow the product if operators use it only once, require PMS mutation as a condition,
cannot approve local third-party tooling, reject the physical layout, or will not pay enough to
cover support and release maintenance.

## Monetization guardrails

- Keep the local processing and data-minimization boundary visible.
- Do not monetize guest data, add advertising, or introduce hidden telemetry.
- Do not claim time savings, error reduction, compliance, or security without measured evidence.
- Do not use payment as proof of safe operational deployment.
- Treat integrations, hosted storage, shared templates, analytics, and accounts as separate product
  and threat-model decisions.
